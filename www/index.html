Arrangement Cell Entry Guide

1 – 64 : Type a pattern number in a bar to play it there. A Drums track plays Drum Machine pattern #N and a Piano track plays Piano Roll pattern #N. On an audio track, type 1 where the clip should start.
(blank) : Silence in that bar. To keep a pattern going, type its number in every bar.
s : Stop. Silences that bar and cuts any audio clip that is still playing.
[e] (click a Bar #) : Set the Song End bar.

The same number in back-to-back bars plays as one run, so a 32- or 64-step pattern carries on from bar to bar. A different number or an empty bar starts the next pattern from its first step. Drums Optional 1–3 and Piano Optional 1–3 are separate tracks with their own volume and pan, so up to 3 patterns can play at once in each group. Double-click a cell to open that pattern in its editor. Audio tracks come from the Splitter, Effects Processing or Bounced tab (✕ Remove takes one out). M / S mute or solo a track. See the Help tab for examples.

      How the Beat Grid retriggers: rows and columns

      Only for 16 slices. The grid has 16 columns, one for each slice, so it is greyed out unless 16 Slices is chosen above. Pick another count (or clear the slices) and it greys out again.

      Columns are time. The 16 columns are the 16 steps of one bar, from left to right, in four groups of four (one group per beat).

      Rows are the beats of your sample. Row 1 is beat 1 (slices 1–4), row 2 is beat 2 (slices 5–8), row 3 is beat 3 (slices 9–12) and row 4 is beat 4 (slices 13–16).

      A lit square in row R, column C means: at step C, jump to the start of beat R and play on from there. Squares that are not lit simply carry on from where the step before left off, so with no square lit you hear your sample unchanged. Each column holds one lit square at most.

      Examples. Light the first column of each beat group in rows 1, 2, 1, 2 and the bar plays beats 1-2-1-2. Light a whole row across four columns and the start of that beat fires again on every step: a stutter ("1111 2222 3333 4444" is preset 4). Press or drag on the grid to draw squares, press a lit square to erase it.

      Play Once / Loop. Play Once plays the bar one time. Loop repeats it until you press Stop. The file you download or send on is always one single bar, steps 1–16, as long as the region you loaded. This works like the Beat Grid in VirtualDJ; Serato DJ has a similar retrigger idea in its Slicer.

      Upload a finished song as a WAV file or an MP3 (320 kbps). GridWave splits it into Vocals, Drums, Bass and Other, cuts every stem into 64 slices at its loudest peaks, and rebuilds the song across the Arrangement, Drum Machine and Piano Roll. Everything runs in the background, one stem after another. When it is finished, the Splitter, MIDI Identifier, Effects Processing, Bounced and Sliced tabs are left empty: the remix lives on the Arrangement.

          Remix a finished song

          On the Remix tab, upload a finished song and GridWave rebuilds it for you. File types: a WAV file or an MP3 at 320 kbps, the same audio files the Splitter, Effects Processing and Bounced tabs accept.

          1. When you choose the song, GridWave asks whether to detect the tempo automatically or to use a BPM you type. The song is then split into Vocals, Drums, Bass and Other.
          2. Every stem is cut into 64 slices at its loudest peaks, the same way the Bounced tab slices.
          3. Drums are re-sequenced and cut again, then become Drum Machine patterns 1–64 (one per slice). They are layered across the three drum tracks of the Arrangement, and every pattern is used once the song is long enough to hold them all. The three drum tracks are then switched to Punchy (see below).
          4. Vocals are re-sequenced into one file as long as the song (about 15% sound and 85% silence by default), given the default effects and placed on the Arrangement as an audio track.
          5. Other slices that contain sound become Piano Roll sounds and fill Piano Optional 1 only. The other two piano tracks stay empty.
          6. Bass is always re-sequenced into one file as long as the song (about 15% sound by default), placed on the Arrangement as an audio track.

          Sliders work like the Randomize sliders on the Piano Roll and Drum Machine tabs: further right means fuller. They all start at 15%, except Arrangement: drum tracks, which starts at 65%. Each re-sequenced file keeps the song's length, so the rest is silence. With Auto, the tempo is detected and then nudged by a fraction of a BPM so the Arrangement ends at the same moment as the song, and the tempo that was found is written into the Tempo box when the Remix finishes; a tempo you type is kept exactly.

          It overwrites your project. Save first (Songs → Save). Remix replaces the Arrangement, Piano Roll and Drum Machine and leaves the Splitter, MIDI Identifier, Effects Processing, Bounced and Sliced tabs empty. Nothing changes until Remix finishes, and Cancel is always safe. The Cancel Remix button is only on the screen while the percentage is counting up.

          It takes time, from a few seconds for a short clip to a few minutes for a long song. Keep the window open and active while the progress bar runs. Minimizing the window or switching to another browser tab can slow it down or pause it.

          Good to know: the Splitter separates stems by frequency range, so the "drums" are mostly the bright hi-hat and cymbal sounds. The vocals go through the default Effects Processing settings, without the echo, so their silent parts stay silent.

          When it is finished, every waveform tab is empty (Splitter, MIDI Identifier, Effects Processing, Bounced and Sliced, and the Beat Grid), the drum and piano tracks are centred, and the finished panel only has Open Arrangement. The remix is on the Arrangement, the Drum Machine and the Piano Roll.

          Undo Remix (next to Start Remix) brings back your project as it was just before you pressed Start Remix, with everything on all the tabs. Only one step is kept, in memory: it stays until you start a new song, load a project or close GridWave. Running a second Remix replaces it.

          How long can things be?

          GridWave is made for creating and remixing songs, not for DJ sets or long mixes. Staying inside these lengths keeps it quick and keeps your browser from running out of memory.

          Maximum Arrangement length: 10 minutes. The + Bar button stops adding bars once the song reaches 10 minutes. A bar is 4 beats long, so the number of bars depends on the tempo: 300 bars at 120 BPM, 150 bars at 60 BPM, 500 bars at 200 BPM. Quick Start builds 16 bars, and Remix follows the length of the song you upload.

          • Whole songs (Splitter, Remix, Effects Processing): up to about 6 minutes is comfortable. Remix accepts a song from 8 seconds up to 10 minutes, and no more.
          • Loops, samples, vocal takes and stems (Effects, Bounced, Sliced, Beat Grid, audio tracks in the Arrangement): from a fraction of a second up to about 1 minute. For slicing, 1 to 30 seconds gives the cleanest slices.
          • MIDI Identifier listens to only the first 64 steps (4 bars), so a short melody or loop of a few seconds is best.
          • Beat Grid works on the region you chose on the Bounced tab. One or two bars is ideal (about 2 to 4 seconds at 120 BPM).
          • Not suitable: DJ sets, recordings of whole shows, podcasts and audiobooks. Cut the part you want in an audio editor first, then import that.

          Why: audio is held in memory uncompressed, about 21 MB per minute for a stereo file, and the Splitter keeps five copies of it (the original and four stems), so a 6-minute song needs roughly 600 MB there. Phones and tablets run out of memory sooner than computers. If the page slows down, freezes or reloads while you import something, the file was too long for your device.

          Building a song: the Arrangement cells

          On the Arrangement tab every row is one bar (16 steps, 4 beats) and every column is a track. What you type in a cell decides what plays in that bar.

          1. Make a pattern. On the Piano Roll or Drum Machine tab, pick a pattern number (#1 to #64) from the pattern menu and add your notes or beats. Piano and drum patterns are numbered separately, so Drum #1 and Piano #1 are two different patterns.
          2. Put it in the song. On the Arrangement tab, tap a cell and type the pattern's number (for example 1), then press Enter or tap somewhere else. That bar now plays the pattern.
          3. Which pattern plays? A Drums track plays the Drum Machine pattern with that number. A Piano track plays the Piano Roll pattern with that number.
          1 – 64 : play that pattern in this bar. Example: type 3 in Piano Optional 1 at bar 5 and Piano Roll pattern #3 plays in bar 5.
          (blank) : silence in that bar. Leave cells empty for a gap or a break.
          s : stop. Silences that bar and cuts any audio clip that is still playing.
          [e] : click a bar number to put the Song End marker there. Song mode plays up to and including that bar.
          Audio tracks (from the Splitter, Effects or Bounced tab): type 1 in the bar where the clip should start. It plays to its end, even across several bars. Type 1 again later to start it again.

          Example. Drums Optional 1: bars 1–4 = 1, bars 5–8 = 2. That plays Drum pattern 1 for four bars, then Drum pattern 2. Now type 3 in Piano Optional 1 for bars 1–8 to layer a piano part on top, and click bar 8 to set the end marker.

          Long patterns. Type the same number in back-to-back bars and they play as one run: a 32-step pattern takes two bars, a 64-step pattern four. A different number, or an empty bar, starts the next pattern from its first step. There is nothing extra to "extend" or "restart": just type the number in each bar you want to hear.

          Layering. Drums Optional 1–3 and Piano Optional 1–3 are separate tracks, so you can fill the same bar in several of them to play up to three patterns at once in each group. Double-click a cell to open that pattern in its editor.

          Delete a bar, and Undo

          • Delete: on the Arrangement tab, double-click (or double-tap) a bar number and confirm. The bar is removed from every track and the bars below it move up by one, so no gap is left. A single click on the bar number still sets the [e] song end marker (after a very short wait, in case you are about to double-click).
          • The end marker moves with the song: delete a bar above it and it moves up by one with the rest; delete the bar that holds it and it moves to the bar before.
          • Undo: the ↶ Undo button under Bar # (top left of the Arrangement) puts back the bar you deleted last, in the same place, and the bars below move down again. GridWave keeps the last 30 deleted bars, so you can press Undo again and again, newest first. The small number under the button says how many are kept.
          • Audio stems are not cut. A stem keeps its full length; only the cell that starts it moves with its bar. If you delete the bar where a stem starts, it will not play until you type a 1 in a bar again or press Undo (GridWave tells you before you confirm).
          • The Arrangement always keeps at least 4 bars. The deleted bars are remembered until you start a new song, load a project or run a Remix; they are not kept when you close GridWave.

          Song mode vs Bar mode

          The Song | Bar switch at the top decides what Play plays. The step counter (BAR 01:1:01) counts the same way in both, which can make them look alike, but they play different things.

          Song mode

          Plays the Arrangement from bar 1 to the End bar, with all tracks together, using the numbers in the cells. The highlighted row on the Arrangement tab shows the bar that is playing. Use it to hear the whole song. Bounce to Bounced uses the same arrangement.

          Bar mode

          Ignores the Arrangement. It plays only the pattern selected on the Piano Roll tab together with the one selected on the Drum Machine tab, from their first step (if one is longer, the shorter one repeats). A pattern is usually one bar (16 steps) but can be up to four bars (64 steps), so Bar mode repeats for as long as the longer of the two. Use it while you write or tweak a pattern. The Arrangement does not matter and its rows do not light up. In the Bounce menu, Full Bar bounces these two selected patterns (two bars of them) instead of the Arrangement, and Full Song bounces the Arrangement.

          Preview Pattern (on the Piano Roll and Drum Machine tabs) plays just that one tab's pattern and repeats until you tap Stop Preview. A thin amber bar moves across the grid with the sound, so you can see which step is playing; the main Play button shows the same bar in Bar mode.

          Example. You wrote Drum pattern #1 and Piano pattern #2.

          Bar mode: choose Drum pattern #1 and Piano pattern #2 on their tabs and press Play to hear them together on repeat.

          Song mode: type 1 in Drums Optional 1 and 2 in Piano Optional 1 for bars 1–4, click bar 4 to set the end marker, switch to Song and press Play to hear four bars.

          Best way to work: write your patterns in Bar mode, then switch to Song mode once the numbers are in the Arrangement. While listening, use S to solo one track and M to mute another. Keep the End marker on your last bar so the song has no empty tail.

          Loop: On vs Loop: Off

          The Loop button at the top works in both Song and Bar mode.

          • Loop: On (default): when playback reaches the end (the End bar in Song mode, or the end of the longest pattern in Bar mode) it jumps back to the start and keeps going until you press Stop. Best while you are writing.
          • Loop: Off: plays once to the end, then stops by itself. Best for checking how your song or bar ends.
          • Bounce to Bounced renders the song once through the End bar, whatever Loop is set to.

          Saving your work

          GridWave has two ways to save. Both store the same song; the difference is where it goes.

          💾 Songs → Save (save song)

          • Keeps a named copy inside the app, on this device.
          • Quickest way to save and reopen: open Songs, pick a song and tap Load. There is no file to find.
          • It stays on this device only, and is lost if you clear the app's data or uninstall it.

          ⬇ Save JSON / Load JSON

          On a phone these are inside Songs as Export file and Import file.

          • Creates a .json file you can keep anywhere, email, or move to another phone or computer.
          • Use it as a backup, or to carry a song between devices. Load JSON (Import file) brings it back.
          • The file can be large because the audio is stored inside it.

          Which one? Use Save song for everyday work. Use JSON before you uninstall, clear app data, or switch devices.

          Both include: tempo and master volume, the Arrangement (tracks, mute/solo, volume, pan and audio tracks), Piano Roll and Drum Machine patterns, the sounds you loaded into the Piano Roll (including the ones a Remix makes), drum samples and volumes, the step counts, and your presets.

          Neither includes: work in progress on the Splitter, MIDI Identifier, Effects, Bounced and Sliced tabs (send what you want to keep to the Arrangement first). That includes the stems a Remix leaves in the Splitter and Bounced.

          Autosave: the Arrangement and patterns are also saved automatically as you work, but audio is not, so use Save song for anything that contains audio.

          Sliced tab: green bars, the silent filler and re-ordering

          • Green bars on every slice. Drag the bright green bars on a slice's waveform to choose which part of the slice is kept. Press on the left half of the waveform to move the Start bar, or on the right half to move the End bar. While you hold a bar the whole slice is shown; when you let go, the waveform zooms in on the part you kept. The Start % and End % sliders and number boxes below the waveform update as you drag, and you can still use them the same way as before.
          • One-second silent filler. Every set of slices comes with a 1-second silent sample (the gray Silence card after the last slice). It starts unticked, so it is not in your pattern until you tick In pattern on its card (or press + Silence under Add Slice Block). Its Copies menu goes up to ×30, so you can use it as a filler between slices (for example ×4 for four seconds of silence). It is not included in Bulk Apply FX, Bulk Export or the → buttons.
          • In pattern and Copies. Untick a slice to drop it from the pattern, or set its Copies (×1 to ×10) to have it appear more often. Randomize pattern shuffles only the blocks that are ticked (the silence too, once you tick it).
          • No clicks or pops. The sequence plays as one smooth piece. Where two blocks that were not side by side in the original meet, GridWave blends the join over a few thousandths of a second, and playback starts and stops with a tiny fade, so re-ordering, randomizing, playing and stopping all stay clean.
          • Bulk Export All Slices uses the Start % and End % you see on the page at the moment you press the button, so what you see is what you export.

          Bounced tab: the green bars and the Beat Grid

          Bouncing and downloading. Bounce to Bounced on the Arrangement tab (and on the Piano Roll and Drum Machine tabs) renders the sound and puts it on this tab. It does not save a file by itself. Here, Download WAV and Download MP3 save the part between the green bars, named after the item (for example Arrangement_Song_(8_Bars).wav, with _region added when you narrowed the bars). An MP3 takes a few seconds to make, and a message shows how far it has got. On a computer the file goes to your Downloads folder, and a note offers Save again in case your browser blocked it. On a phone a share sheet opens, so you can save the file or send it to another app.

          The green bars on the waveform choose the region. Drag the left bar to set where the region starts and the right bar to set where it ends. Only the bright part between the bars is played, downloaded, sliced and sent to the other tabs; the dim part outside them is ignored. Moving a bar clears any slice preview, because those slices were found inside the old region.

          Beat Grid (retrigger) — only for 16 slices

          When it is on. The Beat Grid is always at the bottom of the Bounced tab, but it is greyed out until you choose 16 Slices, because the grid is built for 16 steps. With 4 or 8 slices, or none, it stays greyed out. Once 16 slices are chosen, press Load 16 slices sample into Beat Grid to bring the sliced region in, light some squares, then press Play.

          Columns are time. The 16 columns are the 16 steps of one bar, from left to right, in four groups of four (one group for each beat).

          Rows are the beats of your sample. Row 1 is beat 1 (slices 1–4), row 2 is beat 2 (slices 5–8), row 3 is beat 3 (slices 9–12) and row 4 is beat 4 (slices 13–16).

          A lit square is a retrigger. A lit square in row R and column C means: at step C, jump to the start of beat R and play on from there. A step with no lit square simply carries on from where the step before it ended, so with nothing lit you hear your sample unchanged. Each column holds at most one lit square. Press or drag on the grid to light squares, and press a lit square to switch it off.

          Examples. Light row 1 in the first column of each group and the whole bar repeats beat 1 four times. Light rows 1, 2, 1, 2 in those columns to play beats 1-2-1-2. Light row 1 in columns 1–4, row 2 in columns 5–8, row 3 in columns 9–12 and row 4 in columns 13–16 for the classic stutter “1111 2222 3333 4444”, or reverse the rows for “4444 3333 2222 1111”.

          Presets, Play Once and Loop. There are 10 presets (use the arrows or the menu). Drawing on top of a preset turns it into your own grid. Play Once plays the bar one time; Loop repeats it until you press Stop. Changes you make while it plays are heard straight away. Switching from Loop to Play Once lets the bar that is playing finish, then stops.

          Exporting. What you download or send is always one single bar, steps 1–16, as long as the region you loaded. Use the buttons under the grid (WAV, MP3, Arrangement, Splitter, MIDI ID, Effects, Bounced), the same places as the region above. Every jump is smoothed and the file starts and ends softly, so neither the retriggers nor the edges of the file click.

          This follows the Beat Grid effect in VirtualDJ (see the VirtualDJ forum post on how it works); Serato DJ's Slicer pads are a related retrigger idea.

          Effects Processing: Stereo Delay and Chorus Depth

          • Stereo Delay adds one echo, about a quarter of a second after the sound. The slider is how loud the echo is, and 0% switches it off.
          • Chorus Depth thickens and widens the sound with a slightly delayed copy that wobbles gently and is spread to the left and the right. The slider is how much of it you hear. 0% switches it off, and then the sound is exactly what it would be without it. A mono recording becomes stereo while the chorus is on. It is part of Play Processed, Download, every Send button and Bulk Apply FX, and it answers instantly while audio is playing.
          • Loop (next to Stop) plays the processed audio again and again until you press Stop. It is off when GridWave starts, and you can switch it on or off while the audio is playing.
          • There is no Reverb Mix slider any more. It did not change the sound, so it was taken out. For a real reverb, use DJ Effects → Reverb at the bottom of the tab.
          • The presets Warm Tape, Space Echo and Vocal Punch now include a little chorus, so they sound slightly wider than before. Clean Studio and Lo-Fi Radio have none.

          DJ Effects: Flanger, Reverb and Wah-wah

          At the bottom of the Effects Processing tab, in the style of the effects in DJ software. All three are off until you tick On, they run after the effects above them, and they are included everywhere the Effects rack is used: Play Processed, Download, every Send button, and Bulk Apply FX on the Sliced tab.

          • Flanger: a short, sweeping delay that gives the “jet plane” swoosh. Rate is how fast it sweeps, Depth how far, Feedback how metallic and ringing it sounds, Mix how much you hear.
          • Reverb: adds space, like a room or a hall. Size is how long it rings, Damping how dark and soft the tail is, Mix the balance between the original and the reverb.
          • Wah-wah: a filter that sweeps up and down by itself, like a wah pedal. Rate is the speed of the sweep, Depth how wide it is, Resonance how sharp and vocal it sounds, Mix how much you hear.

          Good to know: the audio keeps its length, so a reverb tail is cut off where the audio ends. To let it ring out, end the audio with some silence, for example the 1-second silent slice on the Sliced tab. With all three switched off, the audio is exactly as it was.

          MIDI Identifier sliders

          • Note Sensitivity and Frame Sustain are the two sliders to try first. Move them until the detected notes look right.
          • Min Note (ms) is the one to use last, only if you cannot get good MIDI results from the other two. It sets the shortest note the tab looks for. Values shorter than one 16th note at your tempo (125 ms at 120 BPM) change nothing; longer values make each detected step longer, which merges fast notes together and steadies the pitch guesses.

          Drum Machine: samples assigned and the Sample Browser

          At the bottom of the Drum Machine tab, under the tracks, is a panel called Samples assigned & Sample Browser. Press its title to fold it away when you need the room.

          • Samples assigned is a numbered key, 01 to 08, for the eight drum tracks of the pattern on screen. It shows the full name of the sound on each track (the label beside each track in the grid can be cut short), or the name of the built-in sound, such as Synth Kick, when no file is loaded. Every pattern has its own sounds, so the key changes when you pick another pattern. The ▶ button plays that sound, and ↺ puts the built-in sound back on a track that has a loaded file.
          • Sample Browser lets you look through your audio files, hear them and put them on a track. Open folder… shows a folder of sounds: click a folder name to go in, and .. goes back up. Add files… picks one or several sound files, and you can also drag files or a whole folder onto the browser. Phones and tablets cannot open a folder from a web page, so there the folder button is hidden and you use Add files… (select several at once). The Filter box narrows a long list.
          • Preview: click a file, then press Preview (or double-click it). If the pattern is playing, the preview plays on top of it, so you can judge the sound against your beat. Press Stop, or the same file again, to stop it. Previewing changes nothing.
          • Assign: with a file selected, press 1 to 8 under Assign to track to put that sound on that track, for the pattern on screen only. The key and the grid update at once. With the file list clicked, the keyboard works too: ↑ and ↓ move through the files, Enter or Space previews, Backspace goes up a folder, and the keys 1 to 8 assign.
          • The WAV/MP3 button on each track in the grid still works as before and shares the same result.
          • File names are shown without the .wav or .mp3 ending wherever a loaded file is named: the key, the track labels, the Piano Roll sound menu, and the Splitter, MIDI Identifier, Effects and Remix file labels.
          • Quick Start never replaces a loaded sound on the Drum Machine or the Piano Roll. See Quick Start above.

          Punchy drum tracks

          Each of the three drum tracks on the Arrangement tab has a ⚡ Punchy button under its M and S buttons. Switch it on and the whole track sounds tighter and harder: more weight in the low end, a little more snap at the top, and a compressor and soft limiter that make the hits hit harder without getting much louder.

          • Default: off in a new song. When a Remix finishes, all three drum tracks are switched on, because re-sequenced drum slices benefit most. Turn any of them off with its button.
          • You can switch it on and off while the song is playing and hear the change at once.
          • Play, Bounce to Bounced and the bar and track exports all use it, and it is saved with your song (Songs, Save JSON and the automatic save).
          • It works on the three drum tracks only. Piano tracks and audio stems have Pre-master instead (see below).

          Pre-master: piano tracks and audio stems

          Each piano track and each audio stem on the Arrangement tab has a Pre-master button under its M and S buttons (the drum tracks have Punchy there). It puts a gentle compressor on that one track, the kind you would use before mastering: the loudest notes come down a little and the quieter parts come up, so the track sounds more even and a bit denser, and about as loud as before.

          • What it sets: threshold −9 dB, a soft knee and a release of 35 ms. The ratio is 2:1 and the attack 0.5 ms, as on the compressor it copies, and the output level stays at 0 dB.
          • Default: off, also after a Remix. It suits the Bass and Vocals audio tracks and the Piano tracks.
          • You can switch it on and off while the song is playing and hear the change at once. Play, Bounce to Bounced and the bar and track exports all use it, and it is saved with your song (Songs and Save JSON; the automatic save keeps it for the piano tracks).
          • It works on one track at a time. Drum tracks use Punchy instead.

          Export every bar or every track

          On the Arrangement tab, press Export Bars / Tracks… (next to Bounce to Bounced) and choose what to save as WAV files:

          • Every bar: one WAV for each bar of the song, from bar 1 to the [e] end marker, named GridWave_Bar_01.wav, GridWave_Bar_02.wav and so on. The song is rendered once and cut exactly on the bar lines, so a note or an echo that rings across a bar line carries on at the start of the next file. It is the mix as you hear it, so muted tracks stay out.
          • Every track: one WAV for each track that has something in bars 1 to End: the Drums Optional tracks, the Piano Optional tracks and every audio stem, named like GridWave_Track_01_Drums_Optional_1.wav. Each file is as long as the whole song, so the files line up when you load them into another program. Each track keeps its volume, its pan and its Punchy or Pre-master setting; Mute and Solo are ignored, so a muted track is still exported. Tracks with nothing in them are left out.
          • ZIP: ticked by default, so you get one download. Untick it to save separate WAV files instead (your browser may ask whether it can download several files).

          A stereo WAV is about 10 MB per minute, so a long song with many tracks makes a big export. Keep to the lengths in How long can things be? above, and use the progress bar and Cancel button in the dialog if you change your mind.

          Live changes, playback lines and pattern numbers

          • Effects Processing: while audio is playing, move any slider or pick a preset and you hear it straight away, with no need to press Play Processed again. Pitch, Output Gain, the High-Pass and Low-Pass filters, the Compressor, the echo and the Chorus answer instantly. The DJ effects (Flanger, Reverb, Wah-wah) are calculated in the background, so a change to one of them arrives a moment later (longer for long audio) and slips in smoothly from the same spot while it keeps playing.
          • Playback line: a thin white line moves across a waveform while it plays: the Splitter stems, the MIDI Identifier, Effects Processing, Bounced, the Beat Grid, each slice on the Sliced tab and the re-ordered sequence.
          • Play button: the main Play button turns amber and pulses while the song is playing, and goes back to white with green letters when you press Stop (or when the song ends).
          • Pattern menus: on the Piano Roll and Drum Machine tabs, the numbers of patterns that already hold notes are highlighted in green in the menu, and the closed menu is green while the pattern you are on holds notes. Empty patterns keep the normal colour. On a phone or tablet the menu opens as a grid of numbers (a panel from the bottom of the screen), because the phone's own menu cannot show colours: the green numbers hold notes, and you tap a number to choose it.

          Quick Start: a ready-made song from a genre

          Quick Start is on the Arrangement tab. Press Quick Start… in the bar at the top of that tab and a window opens. Choose a genre and a sub-genre, then use the tick box in the window to choose how it is applied, and press the button at the bottom. There are two ways:

          The normal way (box not ticked): Quick Start starts a new song. The button reads Clear everything and apply Quick Start. It first clears everything, exactly like the red New Song button: the Arrangement, every Piano Roll and Drum Machine pattern, all audio stems, and the Splitter, MIDI Identifier, Effects Processing, Bounced, Sliced, Beat Grid and Remix content. Saved presets and saved songs are kept. The window tells you this before anything happens, and Cancel leaves everything as it is. To keep your current song, use Songs → Save first.

          Only replace the Drums and Piano (box ticked). Tick Only replace the Drums and Piano, starting at the bar I am in when you want to keep the rest of your song. Nothing is cleared, and the button changes to Replace drums and piano from bar N.

          • Starting bar. The window opens with the last Arrangement cell you clicked filled in as the starting bar. Change the number if you like. Quick Start writes its drum and piano cells from that bar onward (16 bars, or 15 for a 3/4 waltz) and clears only the Drums and Piano cells inside those bars. Everything before the starting bar, everything after the last bar it writes, and every audio stem track stay exactly as they are. The [e] end marker moves out if the new bars go past it, and is never moved back.
          • Your loaded sounds stay. Quick Start writes the squares and notes, never the sounds, so a WAV or MP3 you loaded on a Drum Machine track is never replaced. On the Piano Roll, a pattern that uses a sound you loaded keeps it; patterns on a built-in sound get the genre’s built-in sounds.
          • What does get replaced. The squares in Drum Machine patterns 1–7 and the notes in Piano Roll patterns 1–16. Those pattern numbers change everywhere, so a bar outside the starting range that plays Piano pattern 3, say, now plays the new pattern 3. If a grid needs a different length (the Piano Roll goes to 64 steps for 4/4, and both go to 48 steps for a 3/4 waltz), it is changed to fit. The window tells you before you apply.
          • Tempo. Not changed, unless you also tick Also set the tempo to the BPM above.
          • Limits. A song can be 10 minutes long at most, so bars that would go past that are cut off, and the window says so. If the starting bar is already past the limit, the button is switched off until you choose an earlier bar. It cannot be used while a Remix is running.
          • Cancel leaves everything exactly as it is.

          Either way, GridWave fills the Arrangement (a 16-bar song), the Piano Roll (harmony, bass and lead patterns 1–16) and the Drum Machine (patterns 1–7). A normal Quick Start also sets the tempo to a range that suits the genre.

          The links next to the genre in that window are for reference only: they open a page about the social dances that go with it, and they do not change anything. Each sub-genre is a ready-made drum and harmony style for that genre (some genres also have 3/4 waltz-time sub-genres).
